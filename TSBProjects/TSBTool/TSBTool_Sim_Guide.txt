=== 1. WHAT A SIMULATED GAME USES ===

When you play a game, or watch the computer play one, the game uses the players' RATINGS
(RS, RP, MS, HP, ...) to decide what happens during the plays. 
When the computer SIMULATES a game -- the season's other games, or a game
you choose to sim -- it uses a separate set of values instead: the SIM values ('Sim attributes' on
TSBToolSupreme's player form, the [..] at the end of each player line in TSBToolSupreme's text).

So:
  * Changing ratings does NOT change simulated results. Changing sim values does.
  * A player's sim values should match his ratings, or he'll play like two different players.
    'Auto Update Sim' (updated 2026, section 7) works them out from the ratings for you.

There are two kinds of sim values:
  * Ability values, 0-F (hex 0-15): how good the player is at something -- running, passing,
    catching, kicking. Higher is better.
  * Shares, 0-255: who gets the ball (or the credit) on a play, compared with his teammates.
    See section 2.

Team values: each game also has a team 'SimData' value for the team's defense. TSB1 and TSB2: its
rush defense and pass defense (and in TSB1 a run/pass preference). TSB3: a rush and pass defense
BONUS, on top of what its defenders give (section 6). There is no team offense sim value in any of
the games: a simulated offense uses its players' sim values.


=== 2. SHARES: HOW THE GAME PICKS WHO GETS THE BALL ===

For a carry, a pass target, a sack or an interception, the game rolls a number from 0 to 255 and
walks down the players in order, subtracting each player's share. The player the number runs out
on gets the credit for that game stat. So:

  * Each share is that player's chance out of 256. A share of 64 = about 25% of the time.
  * A team's shares should ADD UP TO 255 -- Tecmo built the stock teams that way.
  * If they add up to LESS, the rest goes to a fallback player (which one depends on the game).
  * If they add up to MORE, the players early in the order use up the 256 and the ones at the end
    get little or nothing. The values are not scaled.

Example: a defense whose sack shares are RE 116, NT 30, LE 25, ... adding up to 255 gives the RE
116/256 = 45% of the team's sacks.

Sack and INT shares only decide WHICH defender gets the credit. They don't make the defense get
more sacks or interceptions -- the team's pass defense (TSB1, TSB2) or its starting defenders
(TSB3) do that.


=== 3. TSB1 (NES, SNES) ===

The NES game, SNES TSB1 and the 32-team NES hacks all run the same sim engine.

QB (3 values)
  Rush       0-F  Yards on QB runs and scrambles.
  Pass       0-F  Completions, and fewer interceptions.
  Scramble   0-F  ('Sim pocket') Chance the QB runs on a pass play. Rare: at most 3 in 256 pass
                  plays whatever the value.
RB / WR / TE (4 values)
  Rush / Returns  0-F  Yards on runs -- and on kick and punt returns when he's the returner.
  Catch           0-F  Completions when he's the target.
  YAC             0-F  ('Sim yards per catch') Yards after the catch. (Not punt returns -- older
                       TSBToolSupreme labels got this wrong.)
  Target share    0-F  How often he's the target of a pass, compared with his teammates.
Defenders: RE NT LE ROLB RILB LILB LOLB RCB LCB FS SS (2 values each)
  Sack share  0-255   His share of the team's sacks (section 2), in that order.
  INT share   0-255   His share of the team's interceptions, in the same order.
                      The 11 shares should add up to 255; anything left over goes to the SS --
                      giving every defender 1 sends about 96% of the sacks and INTs to the SS.
K / P
  Kicking  0-F  Field goal and extra point range and accuracy.
  Punting  0-F  Punt distance.

RETURN_TEAM (SNES and Genesis): the 1st and 2nd players must be a RB, WR or TE and the 3rd a RCB,
  LCB, FS or SS, as in every stock team. A DB returner goes in the 3rd slot.
  - The game treats the 3rd player as a defensive back: a WR there takes a starter's place in
    simulated games (a WR3 the RB2's, a WR4 the WR1's) and shows DB stats on the player screen.
  - DB1 in the 3rd slot returns every kickoff and DB2 every punt in simulated games, whatever
    KR and PR say.
  - All defenders on the return team share one injury flag. If every returner is out the SNES
    game freezes, which happened with DB1, DB2, RCB.
  A RETURN_TEAM line that breaks this is an error and the return team is left unchanged. A KR or
  PR who is not on the return team joins it in the matching slot (RB/WR/TE in the 1st or 2nd, a
  starting DB in the 3rd); anyone else is an error.
  When a ROM is loaded, a return team that already breaks the pattern is fixed and listed in a
  warning: the RB/WR/TE already there keep their slots (WR3, WR4, RB2, RB3 fill gaps), a starting
  DB already there takes the 3rd slot (else RCB), and a DB1/DB2 or other defender who returned
  kicks or punts hands that job to the 3rd-slot DB. Save the ROM to keep the fix.

Team SimData = 0x<rush defense><pass defense><preference>
  Rush defense  0-F  Stops the run (and kick/punt returns).  Higher = better defense.
  Pass defense  0-F  Stops the pass; also more sacks and interceptions.
  Preference    0-3  How often the team passes, 0 = 50/50, 1 = 60% run, 2 = 60% pass, 3 = 70% pass; 

Player condition (bad / average / good / excellent during a season) shifts a player's ability
values by -2 to +4 before each simulated game.


=== 4. GENESIS TSB1 ===

The same values as section 3, with these differences:
  * Scramble: Genesis ignores this value (its scramble check uses the QB's Pass value instead).
    Either way it's at most 3 in 256 pass plays.
  * The stock Genesis game has three sim bugs (section 8): preference 1 plays as heavy PASSING,
    the team rush/pass defense values barely count, and season receiving TDs are lost from the
    stats. TSBToolSupreme's 'GENESIS_FIX_SIM_BUGS' command/option fixes all three.


=== 5. TSB2 (SNES TECMO SUPER BOWL II) ===

TSB2 has three seasons -- 1992, 1993 and 1994 (TSBToolSupreme's SEASON 1, 2 and 3) -- each with its own
rosters, ratings and sim values. The season you start in the game decides which set is used.

QB (3 values)
  Run share        0-255  His share of the team's run plays (section 2). Keep QB2 at 0 (below).
  Rush             0-F    Yards on QB runs and scrambles.
  Pass / Scramble  one hex byte: 1st digit Pass 0-F (higher -> more completions, fewer INTs and sacks),
                   2nd digit Scramble -- chance the QB runs on a pass play:
                     0 = 20% (most)   1 = 10%   2 = 5%   3 = 3% (least)   4 = never
                     5-F = 8% .. 33% (not used by Tecmo)
RB / WR / TE (4 values)
  Run share       0-255  His share of the team's run plays.
  Rush / Returns  0-F    Higher -> more yards on runs, and on kick and punt returns when he's the returner.
  Target share    0-255  His share of the team's pass targets.
  YAC / Catch     one hex byte: 1st digit yards after the catch 0-F, 2nd digit catching 0-F (higher is better).
Defenders: RE NT LE RE2 NT2 LE2 ROLB RILB LILB LOLB LB5 RCB LCB DB1 DB2 FS SS DB3 (3 values)
  Sack share  0-255  His share of the team's sacks, in roster order.
  INT share   0-255  His share of the team's interceptions -- in REVERSE roster order (DB3 first).
  Tackles     0-F    How often he makes the tackle, compared with his teammates.
K / P
  Kicking  0-F  Field goals and extra points.     Punting  0-F  Punt distance.

Team SimData = 0x<rush defense><pass defense>   (0-F each; higher = better defense)

The shares (run, target, sack, INT) work as in section 2. Leftovers go to: run plays -> RB1;
targets -> a random RB3..TE2; sacks -> a random RE..RILB; INTs -> a random NT2..RCB.

Things to know
  * NEVER give a share of FF (255) to a player. 254 is the most you should give (he'll end up with all the stats 
    for the given 'share'). To the game FF means 'injured / not playing': the player is skipped as if he weren't there.
  * Keep QB2's run share at 0. The game picks the ball carrier from both QBs and the RBs/WRs/TEs;
    a run that lands on the QB who isn't playing is credited to nobody (the yards count against
    the defense, but no player gets them). Tecmo's QB2s all have 0.
  * Player condition does NOT affect the TSB2 sim.
  * A kick return by a tight end isn't credited to anyone. (Tecmo never made a TE a kick returner.)
  * TSBToolSupreme versions older than the one with this guide showed and edited SEASON 1's sim values for
    all three seasons, and swapped the ROLB/LOLB and RILB/LILB sim values. Texts saved with those
    versions carry those mistakes; run Auto Update Sim on them before applying.


=== 6. TSB3 (SNES TECMO SUPER BOWL III) ===

TSB3 runs TSB2's sim engine, so its player sim values mean the same as section 5 -- with these
differences:

  * Scramble (2nd digit of Pass / Scramble) goes the OTHER way round:
        0 = 3% (least)   1 = 5%   2 = 10%   3 = 20% (most)   4 = never   5-F = 8% .. 33%
  * Its own tables: Rush values count for less than in TSB2, Pass/Catch for a bit more.
  * Team defense: TSB3 works out each team's rush and pass defense from its starting defenders --
    the front 7 for rush defense (4-3 teams: RE NT LE RE2 + ROLB RILB LILB; the 3-4 teams Bills,
    Patriots, Steelers, Packers and Panthers: RE NT LE + 4 LBs), plus RCB LCB FS SS for pass
    defense -- and adds a hidden per-team bonus. The backups don't count.
    Each defender counts with his FREE AGENT VALUE -- the last digit of 'Face' in the text, 0-F (an
    overall rating Tecmo set by hand; Bruce Smith has E). Changing a defender's ratings alone doesn't
    change it: edit Face's last digit, or run Auto Update Sim, which works it out from the ratings.
    A test season: one team's starters set to 0 went from the best defense to the worst; another's
    front seven set to F made it one of the best.
  * Team SimData is that bonus, and it is NOT TSB2's team SimData:
        Team SimData = 0x<rush defense bonus><pass defense bonus>   (00-FF each, 00 = none)
    e.g. the stock Steelers' SimData=0x0A14 (rush 0A, pass 14). Tecmo gave a bonus to 12 teams, at
    most 0F rush and 14 pass, and those are already big boosts: in a test season a weak defense
    with FF/FF became one of the best, mostly against the run; the pass bonus showed most in sacks.
    FF is safe (it just means the top defense level).
  * Converting a TSB1 or TSB2 roster to TSB3 turns its team SimData into a bonus: only above-
    average digits (9-F) give one, 2 rush / 3 pass per digit above 8 (F -> 0E / 15). Converting
    from TSB3 leaves it out, so the TSB1/TSB2 ROM keeps its own team SimData. A one-byte SimData
    (TSB2's form, or a TSB3 text from an older TSBToolSupreme) is skipped with a warning.
  * Free agents: TSB3 places free agents on teams differently each new season. A signed free agent
    brings his own sim values (from the free agent pool, which TSBToolSupreme edits as 'freeAgents') --
    including his shares, which then count alongside his new team's.
  * The free agent value (last digit of 'Face') is also what TSB3's team defense adds up (above).
  * The FF rule, the QB2 rule and the TE kick return note from section 5 apply to TSB3 too.
    Player condition doesn't affect the TSB3 sim either.


=== 7. AUTO UPDATE SIM ===

The player form's Auto Update button works out every player's sim values from his ratings, and
(TSB1, Genesis and TSB2) every team's SimData -- rush and pass defense -- from its starting
defenders. It replaces values set by hand; a team whose text is missing a starter keeps its team
SimData. (The team forms no longer have their own Auto Update button.) Converting a TSB1 roster to
TSB2 also works out the players' values, but keeps the roster's own team SimData.

TSB1 (NES, SNES, Genesis)
  Players: Tecmonster's community formulas, or TSBToolSupreme's original ones (it asks). Teams:
  Tecmonster's team formula -- the league spread out from 0 to F, run/pass preference kept.
  Sack and INT shares (both choices): worked out a team at a time, fitted to Tecmo's stock shares.
  Sacks go to the front 7 by MS + HP (ends and outside linebackers most, a star pass rusher stands
  out); INTs mostly to the DBs by PI. Each team's shares add up to 256, so the SS only gets his own
  share. After editing defenders by hand, check that the team's 11 sack shares and 11 INT shares
  still add up to about 255.

TSB2 and TSB3
  Models fitted to Tecmo's own sim values in the stock ROMs (TSB2's three seasons, TSB3's one),
  and checked by simulating whole seasons: a season simmed with auto-updated values comes out like
  a stock season (completion %, yards per carry, who gets the carries, catches, sacks and INTs,
  top tacklers, field goals, punts).
  * Works a team at a time: each team's run, target, sack and INT shares add up to 255 (none over
    254), and QB2 gets no run share.
  * Starters get more than backups with the same ratings, as Tecmo did it.
  * A team whose text doesn't have all its players keeps the shares it has (only each player's
    own values are worked out). TSB3's free agents and all-time team keep their shares too.
  * Tecmo set many sim values from each player's real stats that year, so no formula matches them
    all; the model's values are usually within 1 of Tecmo's. Known limits: linebackers get a
    little fewer interceptions than Tecmo's, and kicker/punter values are rough.
  * Applying a TSB1 roster to a TSB2 ROM (with the automatic conversion) works the sack and INT
    shares out with the ROM's own backups (RE2, NT2, LE2, LB5, DB3), which TSB1 doesn't have.
    If you convert first (TSB1 -> TSB2 menu) and apply later, run Auto Update Sim afterwards.
  * TSB2 team SimData: from the 11 starting defenders (RE NT LE, the 4 LBs, RCB LCB FS SS), each
    scored with Tecmonster's rating mix, spread like Tecmo's (average about 7, from 0 to E).
    Tecmo set its team values mostly from each team's real season, which ratings don't show, so
    the worked-out values follow the defenders rather than Tecmo's own numbers.
  * TSB3: every player's free agent value (Face's last digit), which TSB3's team defense adds up, is
    worked out too -- a model fitted to Tecmo's own values (within 1 of Tecmo's for about 3 players
    in 4). The skin tone (Face's first digit) is kept. The TSB2 -> TSB3 conversion does it as well.
  * TSB3 team SimData (the defense bonus) isn't changed; set it by hand if a team's simulated
    defense should be better or worse (section 6).


=== 8. GAME BUGS, AND WHAT TSBToolSupreme CAN FIX ===

Genesis TSB1 -- line 'GENESIS_FIX_SIM_BUGS' (or say Yes when TSBToolSupreme asks while saving)
  * Run/pass preference 1 (heavy rushing) actually plays as heavy passing (about 72% passes).
  * The team rush/pass defense values barely count.
  * Season receiving TDs are lost from the season stats.
  The fix also rebalances the sim so a stock season keeps stock scoring and passing.

TSB2 and TSB3 -- line 'FIX_SIM_BUGS' (or say Yes when TSBToolSupreme asks while saving)
  * Lost interceptions: an interception that lands on an injured defender is credited to nobody.
    About 1 in 20 INTs in TSB2, and about 1 in 8 in TSB3, go missing from the stats. The fix
    (5 bytes) credits every one; a simulated TSB2 season with it credited all 423 INTs thrown.

Not fixed (avoid them instead)
  * TSB2/TSB3: a run that lands on the QB who isn't playing is lost -> keep QB2's run share 0.
  * TSB2/TSB3: a kick return by a tight end isn't credited -> use an RB, WR or DB.
  * TSB2: on one side of each game, QB scramble yards use the other team's QB2 Rush value. Minor.


=== 9. CHECKING YOUR CHANGES: SEASON STATS ===

The best test of sim values is a simulated season. TSBToolSupreme reads the season stats out of the
emulator's save:
  * File -> Save States -> View Season Stats (or '-stats:<file>' on the command line; add '-csv'
    for a spreadsheet). Load the ROM the season was played with to see player names.
  * NES: the battery save (.sav).  SNES TSB1, TSB2, TSB3: the battery save (.srm) -- close the game
    in the emulator first so it's written.  Genesis: an ares save state (.bs1) saved at the
    Season Menu screen.

One season has a lot of luck in it -- two seasons of the same unchanged ROM differ by a few points
here and there. Look for big, consistent differences, not single numbers.
